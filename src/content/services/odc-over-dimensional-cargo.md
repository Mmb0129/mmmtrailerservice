  - question: What counts as ODC?
    answer: ODC stands for Over Dimensional Cargo, also written as an Over Dimensional Consignment. It is a load that exceeds the standard dimensions or the permissible weight set out in the Central Motor Vehicles Rules. The exact thresholds depend on the vehicle and the configuration, so whether your load is ODC is confirmed against the specific trailer rather than guessed from a general figure.
  - question: Do ODC movements need a permit?
    answer: Over dimensional and overweight movements need permission, and what is required depends on the dimensions, the route and the states crossed. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways operates an online permit route for certain single-unit over dimensional and overweight consignments on National Highways. We will tell you what your particular movement needs rather than quote a blanket answer.

Over Dimensional Cargo is any load that goes beyond the standard dimensions or permissible weight for the vehicle carrying it. In practice it means the movement cannot simply be booked and driven: the route has to be worked out and permissions obtained before anything moves.

The planning matters as much as the trailer. Road width and turning radius, bridge load limits, gradients, and overhead cables and clearances all decide whether a given route is usable — and the answer is sometimes a longer way round.
